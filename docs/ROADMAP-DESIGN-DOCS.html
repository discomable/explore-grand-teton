Remaining roadmap: proposed builds

Design specs for every feature still open on the Teton render. Written after a hard build session — so each one leads with the failure mode it must avoid, not just the happy path. The core world is shipped and live; these are the deliberate next moves.

Status Proposed Blocked Partly shipped Shipped

The governing constraint

Nothing that isn't world-anchored. Every effect must be fixed to world or object space. Screen-space passes (a vignette, low-res bloom, SSAO, SSR) crawl as the camera moves and read as artifacts sliding across the terrain — three separate features were pulled this session for exactly this. If an effect can't be made stable in world space, it doesn't ship.

Interactive features are verified in-sim, not in a screenshot. A held map you raise, a bridge you drive across, a waterfall on streamed canyon relief — headless captures can't confirm these. The loop that works: implement → drive/look in the sim → iterate → then commit.

01 · M6

Bridges — unified drivable surface

Shipped

Roads and trails cross water on real bridge decks that the car and the walking player ride across — with the road, the deck, and the physics all agreeing on one height.

Why v1 failed

The road ribbon (a graded visual surface), the bridge deck (separate geometry), and the car physics (raw terrain height) were three surfaces that never agreed. The deck rendered at the wrong level and oversized, the road floated separately above it, and the car dropped through to the lakebed — then flipped 90° at the deck edge where two wheels hung over the drop.

Approach — one surface, read everywhere

The whole feature collapses to a single idea: the road system publishes one authoritative query, drivableSurfaceAt(x,z) → y | null. RoadRibbon already computes a smoothed longitudinal grade profile per way; expose it as a spatial-hashed, interpolatable surface. BridgeBuilder detects the crossings and builds decks pinned to that same profile — so the deck top is the road running surface, never a separate datum. Then both the visual meshes and the physics (sampleGround for the four wheels + the walking player) read this one query. If there is only one surface, the three can't disagree.

Key decisions

  • Deck top ≡ graded road surface. No independent deck elevation — the v1 mislevel is structurally impossible.
  • Detection: a dry-bounded over-water run ≤ 240 m, plus an opposite-banks test to reject a road that merely clips a pond edge.
  • Deck width = carriageway + parapet only (v1's slab was far too wide).
  • Body tilt clamped to ±24° — already shipped — so any sharp edge leans the car hard but never flips it.

Failure modes to avoid

  • A separate deck datum → the road and deck read at different levels.
  • Physics sampling raw terrain instead of the road surface → the car falls through.
  • Unclamped roll at the deck edge → the 90° flip (fixed).
  • An oversized slab at water level instead of a road-width deck.

02 · M4

Waterfalls — bare rock, clean water

Proposed

Animated cascades at the park's real named falls — Hidden Falls the hero — reading as clean falling water on genuine cliff faces.

Why v1 failed

A 23° steepness gate let falls land on forested hillsides, so the water ribbons draped straight through the trees; and the procedural shader mixed hue and foam noise into a garish iridescent smear. It looked like broken glass, not a waterfall.

Approach — gate hard, prefer few

Place a cascade only where the traced fall line is both genuinely steep (slope > ~0.8, i.e. > 38°) and bare rock — tested against ForestMask.probAt. GNIS points that don't sit on a clean face are skipped, not forced: two convincing falls beat ten bad ones. The shader is desaturated white/blue, low-frequency vertical streaks scrolling in world/UV space (never screen space), with tasteful alpha so it doesn't bleed over background trees, plus a soft base mist puff.

Failure modes to avoid

  • A loose slope gate → water on forest or flat ground.
  • Hue / high-frequency foam terms → the iridescent look.
  • Screen-space scroll → the sheet crawls as you orbit.

03 · M7.4

PBR & image-based lighting

Proposed

Physically-based material response where it actually reads: the car body and glass, snow sheen, and wet granite — without touching the draped-NAIP terrain or the water.

Approach — one sky env-map, routed two ways

Capture a single sky-only cubemap at world origin, re-baked only when the time of day changes materially — so it's world-anchored and, at static time, bakes exactly once. Route it two ways: a PMREM feeds scene.environment, which in r128 auto-lights only Standard/Physical materials — i.e. the car — for free selectivity, restoring its metalness and glass reflections. The raw cube feeds the custom terrain shader a Fresnel sky reflection on steep bare "wet" granite. Snow gets an analytic sun-specular sheen that needs no env-map at all. The terrain keeps its NAIP drape; the water keeps its planar mirror.

Key decisions

  • Throttled bake, not per-frame — static time = one bake, no pop; capture linear radiance so the car tone-maps exactly once.
  • Selectivity is free: only Standard materials consume scene.environment; Lambert terrain/veg/buildings ignore it.
  • Ship in phases: car IBL first (highest, lowest risk) → snow specular → wet-rock reflection.

Failure modes to avoid

  • Baking the env every frame → cost and a visible pop; and distant snow specular that shimmers — roughen it with distance.
  • Double tone-mapping the car (verified safe: it maps once via the fog-fragment hook).

04 · M7.5

Water — depth colour & crisper reflections

Proposed

Shallow shore reads green, deep water reads blue; the reflected range is sharper; and a dormant quality-toggle bug gets fixed along the way.

Approach — depth is already in the builder

Water-column depth is free: the mesh builder already samples both the surface and the bed at every node, so carry surface − bed as a per-vertex attribute and mix a shallow green-teal into a deep blue across it in the fragment. Because the gradient lives on the geometry it is perfectly world-anchored — the opposite of a screen-space effect. For reflections, size the planar RT from the drawing buffer (capped) and pull the oblique clip plane ~0.5 m below the surface to kill the razor-clipped near-black waterline slab. SSR is declined — it's screen-space and would crawl.

Quality preset reflection contract

Boot publishes its one lexical water instance as window.GT_waterBuilder and invokes the quality applicator when it exists. This applies a persisted preset before the asynchronous water bake finishes while retaining the settings module's deferred pass. Ultra and High enable reflections; Balanced and Low disable them, so WaterBuilder._reflectPass sets uReflAmt to zero and avoids reflector work.

Failure modes to avoid

  • SSR (screen-space) — declined outright.
  • A bigger reflection RT with the toggle still dead → weak GPUs can't escape the cost. Fix the wiring first.

Files

terrain/src/WaterBuilder.js app/92-boot.js + app/96-settings.js — published water settings hook

Manual regression checklist — quality presets

  1. Stand near a reflective lake and change presets: select Balanced or Low, then Ultra or High.
  2. Verify the mirror turns off and on immediately with each change.
  3. Reload with the selected preset persisted and verify the mirror remains correct.

05 · M4

Road signs — derived NPS signage

Proposed

Brown park guide signs, road-name plaques, and stop signs — with authentic content derived from data already in the repo, no new dataset.

Approach — derive, don't source

There is no clean dataset of every physical sign, so derive them. Build a road graph from roads.json; at each paved junction, generate a brown guide sign ("Jenny Lake ▸ 3 mi") from the nearest reachable named place/lake in labels.json plus distance and bearing; drop a name plaque at the mouth of each named road; place a stop sign at each detected intersection. Each is world-anchored static geometry — a post plus a canvas-text panel built the same way as the tree billboards — facing the oncoming road direction, seated on terrain.heightAt.

Failure modes to avoid

  • THREE.Sprite billboards → they'd rotate to face the camera and read as crawling. Use fixed yaw-rotated quads.
  • Spamming a sign per segment. Cap to junctions and road mouths.

Blocked on a rebake

Why it's blocked

The OSM ref tag (the route number) is read by build_roads.py but not persisted into roads.json — no feature carries it today. So shields need a small pipeline change to surface ref, a roads rebake, and an R2 re-upload before the sign builder can draw them. Pure follow-on to Road signs.

07 · M7.1

Contact AO & depth of field

Partly shipped

Grounding contact shadows and optional lens depth-of-field — closing out the post-processing milestone.

What shipped, and what's deliberately declined

Baked, world-anchored contact AO is already in: a vertical darkening gradient on building bases and a base-cavity on the tree cards, both baked into geometry — zero runtime cost, MSAA-safe, and impossible to crawl. Screen-space SSAO was declined on purpose: it needs a depth prepass (the vertex-displaced terrain can't feed a MeshDepth override), sacrifices hardware MSAA, and — without a TAA system this pipeline doesn't have — crawls under motion, the exact artifact class this whole project rejects. Depth-of-field is in the same bucket: only worth it if it can be made world-stable, otherwise skipped.

RecommendationConsider M7.1 effectively done; do not chase screen-space SSAO/DoF.
