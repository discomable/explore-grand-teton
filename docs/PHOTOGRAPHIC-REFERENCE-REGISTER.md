# Grand Teton photographic reference register

This register is the visual target for Phase 0. It is deliberately weighted toward
National Park Service (NPS) and U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) material whose location,
season, and usage status can be checked. It is a look-development reference set, not an
asset pack: source images must not be copied into the product unless their individual
rights record permits it.

## Usage rules

- **USGS public domain** means the linked media record explicitly says “Public Domain.”
- **NPS downloadable public domain** means the image must be opened in the NPS media
  gallery and remain downloadable. The [park gallery policy][gallery-policy] says that
  downloadable gallery images are public domain and asks users to credit NPS. Record the
  individual media-detail URL and photographer before ingesting a file.
- **Reference only** means the page is authoritative visual evidence, but its image
  detail does not establish reusable rights. Use it to judge the renderer, not as a
  shipped texture or marketing image.

Every name in backticks is a versioned pose in the `reference` suite. Coordinates are
reproducibility anchors, not claims about the exact photographer position; refine heading,
field of view, weather, and time against the linked image during pose review.

| Summer / autumn / snow | R01, R03–R05, R08–R11 / R06–R07, R13 / R02, R12, R14 |

“Storm” should initially be validated through R12/R14’s diffuse, moisture-heavy
lighting. A later register revision should add a rights-verified NPS storm-front image
before dramatic cumulonimbus, lightning, or rain shafts become acceptance targets.

## Capture protocol

For every mapped pose, store the camera coordinates, heading, pitch, field of view,
time, weather, humidity, season, and quality preset with the capture. Match the
reference in this order:

1. silhouette, horizon, and foreground/midground/background proportions;
2. exposure and white balance;
3. atmospheric separation and shadow density;
4. material response for snow, water, rock, soil, bark, foliage, and structures;
5. fine detail and post-processing.

The source photographs have different cameras, edits, and dates, so pixel-perfect
color matching is not a valid target. The acceptance test is that the simulation
reproduces the scene’s stable physical cues without borrowing a photograph’s unknown
grading artifacts.
